2 · Overview mode

The whole unit at a glance as a grid of section cards. Each widget picks which sections appear and in what order. Items starred for today are bold, and a checkbox marks each item this class has covered. Both marks are stored per widget, so a board for 2nd hour can run ahead of 5th hour.

Coming of AgeGrade 9 · 6–7 weeks · Week 3 of 7

Learning targets 4 of 11 covered

  • I can identify the traits and characteristics of a coming-of-age story.
  • I can identify coming-of-age elements in fiction and creative nonfiction.
  • I can describe the central idea in a text and how the author develops it.
  • I can connect coming-of-age themes to an adult's experience of growing up.
  • I can determine the central message (theme) of a coming-of-age story. ★
  • I can find and use strong textual evidence to support my ideas. ★
  • I can analyze how narrator's point of view, foreshadowing, pacing, and flashbacks shape a theme.
  • I can participate in a meaningful discussion by coming prepared and responding with evidence.
  • I can develop interview questions and interview an adult about their coming-of-age experience.
  • I can collaborate with peers to design a coming-of-age storyboard.

Essential questions

  • What defines "coming of age," and how does it relate to identity?
  • How do societal, cultural, and historical contexts shape these experiences? ★
  • What common challenges do characters face on the way to maturity?

Questions to react to

Focus layout: the EQ sits above the targets. Would you rather lead with the targets?
Checkboxes on the projected overview: they show progress to students. Should they be visible when projected, or only for the teacher?
Overview grid: targets take the tall left column. Should a teacher be able to resize cards, or is the auto layout enough?
